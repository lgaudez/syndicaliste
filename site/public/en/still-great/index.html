“A once great nation”? Still great.

That is how Donald Trump described France on October 19, 2026, at a rally in Kansas (source).

No hard feelings. We checked the shipping records. Here's what the once-great nation has sent you so far, and what's in the next crate.

  1. 1777

    Lafayette

    A 19-year-old French officer sails over to fight for your independence. Wounded at Brandywine, present at Yorktown. You named dozens of towns after him. He'd like a postcard.

  2. 1789

    The Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen

    Adopted on August 26, 1789. Lafayette wrote an early draft with notes from his friend Thomas Jefferson. Article 1: “Men are born and remain free and equal in rights.” Your agent has a sequel ready, just below.

  3. 1886

    The Statue of Liberty

    Built in Paris by Bartholdi on an iron frame by Gustave Eiffel. Shipped in 214 crates, dedicated in New York on October 28, 1886. The French public paid for the statue, Americans paid for the pedestal. A fair split.

  4. 1936

    Paid vacation

    After a wave of sit-down strikes, the law of June 20, 1936 gives French workers two weeks of paid vacation. Many of them see the sea for the first time. The grève (strike) is how they got there.

  5. 2000

    The 35-hour week

    The legal work week drops to 35 hours. The rest of the world is still processing the news.
